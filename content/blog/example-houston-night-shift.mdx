Example: this letter started as a weekly note about people who leave work when the city is already dark. Houston is the place I used for the sample, because the Ship Channel side of town does not pretend the night is gentle. The air stays warm. The feeder roads stay busy. A thriller that begins in a ballroom would be lying to that reader.

The page that belongs with the letter is the [Houston night-shift page](/free-thriller/texas/houston/). Its introduction is marked as example copy on purpose. The story embedded there is not example copy. It is Pump Six, and its own address is the [Pump Six free read](/free-reads/pump-six/). That split matters. The short story should have one home. The city page should explain why a Houston reader might want it tonight, in words that are not pasted from Dallas or Austin.

On the page, an overnight clerk watches a clean black sedan stop at a pump and never buy gas. He is not Ryan Kane. He is someone who has been told not to get involved, which is the kind of instruction that fails the moment you notice a notebook pointed at a car in the dark. If you have ever worked a counter after midnight, you already know the feeling of being the only person who is paid to look and the only person who is not supposed to do anything about what you see.

I am not going to summarize the ending. The story is short enough to read on the page, and then the longer book is waiting. HUNTED is the Book 1 prequel in The Ryan Kane Series.
