Some thrillers begin with a crime. The two in this post begin with something quieter and worse: the discovery that an old one was never really finished.

In The Case Files, my reader email for this round of swaps, I featured two thrillers from fellow authors, Cara Toner and Jemma Stark, next to the Ryan Kane books. This essay gathers that same list. If you would rather see covers first, the whole lineup is also on the [Case Files page](/free-thriller/texas/dallas/).

## Why these two books

In the email I put it this way. One story starts with a single entry in a cabin's guest book. The other starts with a death that was closed too quickly. In both, somebody kept the file open.

That is the pull of a reopened case. You get two stories at once: the one everyone agreed on, and the one that is starting to come loose underneath it. Every new detail has to be checked against the old version, and the old version keeps losing. Nobody in these books gets to start fresh. They inherit a mystery somebody else left unfinished, and the people who left it may still be close enough to notice.

## The Ones Worth Keeping by Cara Toner

![Cover of The Ones Worth Keeping by Cara Toner](/covers/the-ones-worth-keeping.jpg)

**The Ones Worth Keeping** (Cara Toner · A Psychological Thriller)

*Someone is watching them. Playing with them.*

Nicole Smith expected four days with her closest friends at a secluded country getaway. Then an entry in the guest book links their cabin to the unsolved disappearance of Emily Carter. Threatening notes start appearing, phones go missing, and it becomes clear the nightmare didn't begin when they arrived. It began years ago.

[Get The Ones Worth Keeping on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HCH1QPV1)

The hook I pulled for Cara Toner's book was a single line: someone is watching them, playing with them. What makes it a strong pick for this list is the turn at the end of the description. The nightmare didn't begin when the friends arrived. It began years ago, which means the cabin itself is tied to an old, unsolved case. A getaway with your closest friends is supposed to be the easy part of the year. Here, a guest book is enough to change that.

## Cold Hard Murder by Jemma Stark

![Cover of Cold Hard Murder by Jemma Stark](/covers/cold-hard-murder.jpg)

**Cold Hard Murder** (Jemma Stark · A Briana Song Crime Thriller, Book 4)

*She didn't realize the case would chase her back.*

Briana Song and her partner, Buck, take on the reopened death of Terry Russo, a case once closed under suspicious circumstances. A pizza delivery boy confessed, then disappeared, and anonymous tips lead to secret recordings, each one more damning than the last. Book 4 of the Briana Song Crime Thrillers.

[Get Cold Hard Murder on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DHV4463Q)

Cara Toner's book drops a group of friends into an old mystery by accident. Jemma Stark's sends Briana Song and her partner, Buck, into one on purpose. They are working a death that was once closed under suspicious circumstances, with a confession, a disappearance and a trail of anonymous tips to sort through. It is Book 4 of the Briana Song Crime Thrillers, and the hook says it plainly: she didn't realize the case would chase her back.

## Where HUNTED fits

The Ryan Kane novels approach that same unease from the other side of the folder. In HUNTED, the series prequel, Ryan Kane has been sleeping in his car in Phoenix for three weeks. His memory slips, his hands shake, and the same details keep returning: a black sedan, a phone pointed his way, a stranger writing something down. Somebody is keeping a log on him.

These lines are the ones I lifted for the Case Files email, word for word from the book:

> Like someone had just put it there. (HUNTED · Chapter 3)

> Repo man? Cop? Someone tracking me? (HUNTED · Chapter 3)

> He'd been parked two blocks away. Watching through binoculars. (HUNTED · Chapter 3)

> Just sat there. Watching the street. Waiting for sunrise. (HUNTED · Chapter 3)

In Cara Toner's and Jemma Stark's books, someone opens an old file. In HUNTED, Ryan is the file, and he does not know who is writing in it. The prequel is a BookFunnel download.
